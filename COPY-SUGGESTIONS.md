# Copy Analysis & Suggestions: Services Section
**Date:** 2026-05-03
**Section:** `src/components/Services.jsx` + `src/data/projects.js` (services array)
**Copy Score:** 52/100

---

## Executive Summary

The services section is structurally sound — three tiers are well-differentiated and the featured card is a smart design choice. But the copy is operating well below its potential. The heading "Every project is different" is defensive vagueness dressed up as flexibility. The intro talks about Yellow Goat Co. when the buyer is asking about themselves. The card descriptions are passive statements rather than buyer-mirror sentences that trigger recognition.

The good news: the bones are right. The kickers (01/Launch, 02/Sharpen, 03/Custom) have personality. The featured card for Site Redesign is correctly identified as the primary offer. The list items are honest and clear. The fixes are at the headline and opening copy level — the structure doesn't need changing.

## Value Proposition Analysis

```
TARGET CUSTOMER: Service-based business owner with good work but a site
                 that no longer reflects the quality of what they do
PROBLEM: Their site is losing them clients they should be winning
SOLUTION: Helen redesigns the site to close that gap
UNIQUE MECHANISM: One person handles strategy, design, and build together —
                  no handoff, no translation loss
KEY BENEFIT: A site that makes their caliber of work obvious
PROOF: [MISSING — no testimonials or metrics currently in this section]
```

**Gap:** The current services copy never names the mechanism (one person, no handoff) that was correctly identified in the H2 elsewhere. This is Helen's biggest differentiator and it goes unmentioned in the section where a buyer is deciding whether to spend money.

## Headline Recommendations

**Current:** "Every project is different."

**Why it's weak:** It's a defensive hedge. It preempts "why don't you have prices?" rather than earning the buyer's confidence. It's also technically meaningless — every project at every agency is different.

**10 alternatives ranked:**

| # | Headline | Framework | Why It Works |
|---|----------|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | **The scope changes. The standard doesn't.** | BAB | Answers the pricing objection AND communicates quality guarantee in one line. Memorable. |
| 2 | **Three ways in. One standard of work.** | Clarity | Directly maps to the three cards. Sets expectation that all tiers are equally cared-for. |
| 3 | **Pick your starting point.** | Action | The most direct thing you can say. Lets the buyer self-sort without friction. |
| 4 | **Same eye. Right scope for your stage.** | Value | "Same eye" nods to taste/design quality. "Your stage" is buyer-segmenting without being condescending. |
| 5 | **Great work deserves the right engagement.** | Emotion | Flatters the buyer slightly while framing the offering as earned, not generic. |
| 6 | **Strategy, design, build — scoped to what you actually need.** | Specificity | Reintroduces the unique mechanism (one person, all three) without wasting the H2 on the section above. |
| 7 | **One engagement won't fit all. Here's what fits yours.** | Relevance | Conversational. Acknowledges the buyer's concern before they raise it. |
| 8 | **Built to your situation, not a service menu.** | Differentiation | Positions against generic agency packages. |
| 9 | **Choose the scope that fits where you are.** | Clarity/Action | Clean, simple, buyer-empowering. |
| 10 | **Not every business needs the same website.** | PAS lead | Sets up the three cards perfectly as the "solve." |

**Recommended:** `The scope changes. The standard doesn't.` — it's a brand statement, not just a section heading. It's memorable, handles the pricing objection, and creates confidence that a Launch Page isn't somehow lesser work than a Custom Build.

## Section-by-Section Copy Suggestions

### Section Intro Paragraph

**Current:**
> From first conversation to final launch, Yellow Goat Co. turns misaligned positioning and outdated sites into focused, conversion-ready experiences built around real business growth. Not sure which suits you? Reach out and we'll figure it out together..

**Issues:**
- Leads with the brand name, not the buyer's situation
- "Misaligned positioning" and "conversion-ready experiences built around real business growth" are agency-speak, not plain English
- Double period at end ("together..")
- "Not sure which suits you? Reach out" soft-pedals what should be a confident offer

**Recommended:**
> Whether you're launching something new, fixing a site that's fallen behind, or building something from the ground up — every project gets the same level of strategic attention. Not sure where you fit? A short conversation will tell us both.

**Why:** Buyer-first sentence structure. Three scenarios map directly to the three cards. Removes jargon. "Tell us both" is more honest and equally approachable.

**Alt (more direct):**
> Three scopes. One person across all of them — strategy, design, and build, without the handoffs. If you're not sure where to start, a short conversation is enough.

---

### Service Card: Launch Page

**Current:**
> A focused page for a new offer, campaign, or business that needs a credible web presence fast.

**Issue:** Passive and generic. "Credible web presence fast" is table stakes for any web service. Doesn't speak to the buyer's specific fear.

**Recommended:**
> You have an offer worth taking seriously. This gets it in front of people who will — designed and built to convert from day one.

**Alt:**
> For new offers that need to look the part before the audience catches on. Fast without looking rushed.

---

### Service Card: Site Redesign (featured)

**Current:**
> For businesses with an existing audience, but a site that no longer matches the quality of the work.

**Issue:** This is actually the best card description currently — it correctly names the pain. The fix is tightening it and adding more sting.

**Recommended:**
> Your work is strong. Your site is the reason clients hesitate. This fixes the gap — without starting from scratch or losing what's already working.

**Alt:**
> The most common situation: the business has grown, the site hasn't. A full redesign that matches where you actually are now.

---

### Service Card: Custom Build

**Current:**
> A complete website or advanced implementation for brands that need something more distinctive.

**Issue:** "More distinctive" is vague. "Advanced implementation" sounds like jargon. Doesn't explain who this is actually for.

**Recommended:**
> When a template would be a ceiling, not a starting point. Built from scratch around how your brand thinks, moves, and needs to convert.

**Alt:**
> For brands where "close enough" isn't close enough. Multi-page builds, custom interactions, and implementation built entirely to your spec.

---

### Card Kicker Refinements (optional)

The kickers (01 / Launch, 02 / Sharpen, 03 / Custom) are strong. "Sharpen" is the best one — it implies the business is already good and just needs focus. No change needed unless you want to harmonize all three into verb form: Launch / Sharpen / Build.

## CTA Optimization

**Current CTA:** "Reach out and we'll figure it out together." (inline text, no button)

**Issue:** No dedicated CTA in the services section. After reading the cards, the buyer has no immediate action to take from here.

**Recommended additions:**
- Add a CTA button or link after the intro: "Let's talk about your project →"
- Or after the cards: "Not sure which fits? Start with a conversation."

The contact section below handles the form — but a soft "bridge" CTA at the end of services would reduce drop-off for buyers who are ready but uncertain.
